About

NYC Hazard Historian brings New York City hazard events and their documented consequences into one record, with each figure's source, period and status.

Why

New York City Emergency Management publishes a Hazard History and Consequence Tool. The record inside it is rich, but getting a question answered out of it is hard. Thirteen consequence datasets sit on their own pages. A search cannot be linked or shared, because it is a form post. A measure nobody collected can print as a zero: the tool reports 0 percent of students absent during Hurricane Sandy, when schools were closed for a week.

This site rebuilds the record from the same public sources, and shows every figure next to its source, its period and its status: reported, deferred, or not collected. The event pages bring weather records and reported consequences together, with source notes for each measure.

Scope

  • It does not rank events. The measures have different coverage and cannot support a single event ranking. Federal assistance is the sharpest case: it is obligated against a declaration, one declaration can cover fourteen events here, and no page sorts events by it.
  • It does not answer questions in plain language. Search and filter controls show which criteria are active.
  • It does not fill gaps. Unavailable measures are labeled with the reason they are missing.
  • It does not estimate, interpolate or repair a source value. Where a published figure looks wrong, the site shows it as published and flags it.

Built

Python's standard library joins and validates the source data in five pipeline stages. The website uses static HTML, CSS and JavaScript, served from GitHub Pages, with MapLibre GL for maps, CARTO basemaps and Iowa radar tiles.

Independence

No agency reviewed this site.

Credits

Data from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, the National Weather Service, the National Hurricane Center, the Federal Emergency Management Agency, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Environmental Protection Agency, NYC Open Data and the Iowa Environmental Mesonet at Iowa State University. Each source is listed with its grain, its coverage and its caveat on the method page.

Claude was used in development.

Reuse

Code is BSD 3-Clause licensed. Compiled data can be reused with attribution; the source data retain their own terms. Carry a figure's status and period when republishing it.
